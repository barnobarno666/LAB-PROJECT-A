The two reactions retained in this reduced model are:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Reaction 2:}\quad &\mathrm{CO+3H_2 \rightarrow CH_4+H_2O}\\
\text{Reversed reaction 3 (WGS):}\quad &\mathrm{CO+H_2O \rightarrow CO_2+H_2}
\end{aligned}
\]Each reaction consumes one mole of CO per mole of reaction. In a small catalyst slice \(dW\), the CO flow therefore changes by
\[
dF_{\mathrm{CO}}
=
-a r_2\,dW-a r_3'\,dW.
\]Divide by \(dW\) and take the limit as the slice gets very small:
\[
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{CO}}}{dW}
=
-a(r_2+r_3').
\]


Conversion follows from the definition: inlet CO minus the CO remaining, divided by inlet CO.
\[
X_{\mathrm{CO}}(W)
=
\frac{F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}-F_{\mathrm{CO}}(W)}
{F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}}
=
1-\frac{F_{\mathrm{CO}}(W)}{F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}}.
\]Since inlet flow is constant, differentiating gives the local slope of conversion with catalyst mass:
\[
\frac{dX_{\mathrm{CO}}}{dW}
=
\frac{a(r_2+r_3')}{F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}}.
\]
