\section{Model basis}
The bed is treated as a steady, one-dimensional plug-flow reactor. The reduced network retains CO methanation and water-gas shift (WGS):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CO+3H_2} &\longrightarrow \mathrm{CH_4+H_2O} && (r_2),\\
\mathrm{CO+H_2O} &\longrightarrow \mathrm{CO_2+H_2} && (r_{\mathrm W}).
\end{aligned}
\]
At each position, the reduced M4 kinetics give net rates \(r_2\) and \(r_{\mathrm W}\) from local temperature and partial pressures \(p_i=y_iP\). Rates are in \(\mathrm{mol\,kg_{cat}^{-1}\,s^{-1}}\), flows \(F_i\) in \(\mathrm{mol\,s^{-1}}\), and \(a\) is catalyst activity. Integration uses catalyst mass \(W\). For uniform loading \(\rho_B\) in a tube of cross-section \(A_c=\pi D_t^2/4\),
\[
W=\rho_B A_c z,\qquad z=\frac{W}{\rho_B A_c},\qquad W_{\mathrm{tot}}=\rho_B A_cL.
\]

A steady mole balance over a catalyst slice \(dW\) gives \(dF_i/dW=a\sum_j\nu_{ij}r_j\), where \(a\) is catalyst activity. Written for the modeled species:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{CO}}}{dW}&=-a(r_2+r_{\mathrm W}), &
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{H_2}}}{dW}&=a(-3r_2+r_{\mathrm W}),\\
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{CH_4}}}{dW}&=ar_2, &
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{H_2O}}}{dW}&=a(r_2-r_{\mathrm W}),\\
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}{dW}&=ar_{\mathrm W}, &
\frac{dF_{\mathrm{N_2}}}{dW}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

\section{Temperature from the energy balance}
For a differential slice, reaction heat raises the gas sensible enthalpy; heat transfer to the wall removes heat when the gas is hotter than the wall. Thus
\[
\sum_i F_iC_{p,i}\frac{dT}{dW}
=a\left[r_2(-\Delta H_2)+r_{\mathrm W}(-\Delta H_{\mathrm W})\right]
-\frac{Ua_w}{\rho_B}(T-T_w).
\]
Dividing by the gas heat-capacity flow gives the temperature equation integrated by the solver:
\begin{keyequation}
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\frac{dT}{dW}=
\frac{a\left[r_2(-\Delta H_2)+r_{\mathrm W}(-\Delta H_{\mathrm W})\right]
-\dfrac{Ua_w}{\rho_B}(T-T_w)}{\sum_iF_iC_{p,i}}}
\]
\end{keyequation}
Here \(a_w=4/D_t\) is the cylindrical heat-transfer area per bed volume. The formulation uses exothermic heats \(-\Delta H_2=206\) and \(-\Delta H_{\mathrm W}=41\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\). The current implementation uses constant heat capacities and these constant reaction enthalpies.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
\section{Pressure from the Ergun equation}
The Ergun pressure gradient combines viscous resistance (first term) and inertial resistance (second term):
\[
\frac{dP}{dz}=-\left[
\frac{150\mu(1-\varepsilon_b)^2u_s}{D_p^2\varepsilon_b^3}
+\frac{1.75\rho_g(1-\varepsilon_b)u_s^2}{D_p\varepsilon_b^3}
\right].
\]
The reacting-gas properties are recalculated locally:
\[
u_s=\frac{F_{\mathrm{tot}}RT}{PA_c},\qquad
\rho_g=\frac{P\overline M}{RT},\qquad
\overline M=\sum_i y_iM_i,\qquad y_i=\frac{F_i}{F_{\mathrm{tot}}}.
\]
Since \(dz/dW=1/(\rho_BA_c)\), the pressure equation in the solver's catalyst-mass coordinate is
\begin{keyequation}
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\frac{dP}{dW}=-\frac{1}{\rho_BA_c}\left[
\frac{150\mu(1-\varepsilon_b)^2u_s}{D_p^2\varepsilon_b^3}
+\frac{1.75\rho_g(1-\varepsilon_b)u_s^2}{D_p\varepsilon_b^3}
\right]}
\]
\end{keyequation}
Pressure is integrated in Pa; the kinetic partial pressures are evaluated in bar. The pressure loss is coupled to reaction because the local flow, composition, temperature, and density change down the bed.

\section{Integrated solution and plotting}
The numerical problem is the coupled initial-value system
\[
\frac{d\mathbf y}{dW}=\mathbf f(\mathbf y),\qquad
\mathbf y(W)=
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf F(W)^{\mathsf T}&T(W)&P(W)\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T},\qquad
\mathbf y(0)=
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf F_0^{\mathsf T}&T_0&P_0\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T},
\quad 0\le W\le W_{\mathrm{tot}}.
\]
The code integrates this system with the stiff Radau method and samples the solution at 401 evenly spaced catalyst-mass locations. The plotted coordinates are \(z_j=W_j/(\rho_BA_c)\), \(T_j=T(W_j)\), and \(P_{j,\mathrm{bar}}=P(W_j)/10^5\). CO conversion is calculated as
\[
X_{\mathrm{CO}}(W)=1-\frac{F_{\mathrm{CO}}(W)}{F_{\mathrm{CO},0}}.
\]
Differentiating this definition and substituting the CO balance gives
\[
\frac{dX_{\mathrm{CO}}}{dW}
=\frac{a(r_2+r_{\mathrm W})}{F_{\mathrm{CO},0}}.
\]
Therefore, the figures show samples of the integrated profiles, not a separate closed-form solution for \(T(z)\) or \(P(z)\).

\section{Kinetic rate-law audit}
The verification implementations were checked against the printed kinetic equations and their published parameter bases. All partial pressures are evaluated from local mole fractions and total pressure; the literature-model integrators convert reported rates to the common catalyst-mass coordinate. The comparison remains a no-fit transferability check because the catalysts and, for Quindimil, the inlet reaction family differ from Experiment 1.

\textbf{Full M4.} The implementation follows Celoria et al., Eqs. (19)--(21), with the common denominator
\[
D_{M4}=1+\sqrt{K_{H_2}p_{H_2}}+K_{CO}p_{CO}+K_C\frac{p_{CH_4}}{p_{H_2}^2}+K_{CH_4}p_{CH_4}.
\]
Expanding the paper's equilibrium-driving-force forms gives the rates used in code:
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=\frac{k_1}{D_{M4}^2}\left(\frac{p_{CO_2}p_{H_2}^{3/2}}{p_{H_2O}}-\frac{p_{CH_4}p_{H_2O}}{K_{eq,1}p_{H_2}^{5/2}}\right),\\
r_2&=\frac{k_2}{D_{M4}^2}\left(p_{CO}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}-\frac{p_{CH_4}p_{H_2O}}{K_{eq,2}p_{H_2}^{5/2}}\right),\\
r_3^{RWGS}&=\frac{k_3}{D_{M4}^2}\left(p_{CO_2}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}-\frac{p_{CO}p_{H_2O}}{K_{eq,3}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the methanation rates and $r_3^{RWGS}$ is the paper's reverse water-gas-shift rate. The public reactor rates use forward WGS, so its signed rate is $r_W=-r_{RWGS}$. Kinetic and adsorption temperature shifts use the paper's Arrhenius and van't Hoff forms, respectively. Source: \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2025.162113}{Celoria et al. (2025)}.

\textbf{Kopyscinski.} Equations (25)--(26) in the published corrigendum are used, including the corrected $\sqrt{p_{CO}}$ adsorption factor:
\[
D_K=1+K_C\sqrt{p_{CO}}+K_{OH}\frac{p_{H_2O}}{\sqrt{p_{H_2}}},\quad
r_{CH_4}=\frac{k_1K_C\sqrt{p_{CO}p_{H_2}}}{D_K^2},\quad
r_W=\frac{k_2\left(K_\alpha p_{CO}p_{H_2O}-p_{CO_2}p_{H_2}/K_{eq}\right)}{\sqrt{p_{H_2}}D_K^2}.
\]
The paper's second rate is positive in the forward WGS direction; the verification integrator converts it to the internal RWGS stoichiometric sign. The Arrhenius and adsorption-constant correlations are the published ones, with pressure in bar and rates in mol kg$_{cat}^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$. Source: \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2010.05.001}{Kopyscinski et al. corrigendum}.

\textbf{Quindimil.} The implemented shared denominator and expanded rates are
\[
D_Q^{impl}=1+K_{HCOO}p_{CO_2}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}+K_{OH}\frac{p_{H_2O}}{\sqrt{p_{H_2}}}+K_{CO}p_{CO},\quad
\begin{aligned}
r_{RWGS}&=\frac{k_4}{(D_Q^{impl})^2}\left(p_{CO_2}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}-\frac{p_{CO}p_{H_2O}}{K_{eq,RWGS}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}}\right),\\
r_{CH_4}&=\frac{k_6}{(D_Q^{impl})^2}\left(p_{CO}\sqrt{p_{H_2}}-\frac{p_{CH_4}p_{H_2O}}{K_{eq,COmeth}p_{H_2}^{5/2}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The reversible driving forces are algebraically equivalent to the paper's RWGS and CO-methanation forms; the gas-phase CO net formation is $r_{CO,net}=r_{RWGS}-r_{CH_4}$. Table 4 values are shifted with the paper's activation-energy/adsorption-enthalpy correlations; pressures are converted from bar to atm and rates from mol g$_{cat}^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$ to mol kg$_{cat}^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$. One qualification matters: Appendix A, Eq. (A3.15), contains an additional standalone $\sqrt{K_{H_2}p_{H_2}}$ term in the shared denominator, but Table 4 reports only the composite $K_{HCOO}=K_{CO_2}\sqrt{K_{H_2}}$ and does not tabulate an independent $K_{H_2}$. The current code retains the reported composite term and omits that standalone contribution. Therefore this is a reduced/lumped reading of the published model, not an exact reproduction of the full printed denominator. In addition, the model was fitted to CO$_2$/H$_2$ experiments, so applying it to the CO/H$_2$ inlet is a feed-scope extrapolation. Source: \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcou.2022.101888}{Quindimil et al. (2022)}.

\begin{keyequation}
\small The signs are consistent once directions are made explicit: the M4 kinetics report RWGS and the reactor uses WGS ($r_W=-r_{RWGS}$); Kopyscinski's second rate is forward WGS and is negated for the reactor's RWGS stoichiometric column; Quindimil's pair is represented as RWGS plus CO methanation. The M4 equilibrium constants in this code use the documented constant-$\Delta H$ thermodynamic closure, rather than claiming a verbatim reproduction of an unreported paper-specific numerical equilibrium correlation.
\end{keyequation}

{\Large\bfseries\color{navy} Defining the ratio and space-time sweep\par}
\vspace{3pt}{\color{copper}\rule{22mm}{1.6pt}}\par
\vspace{8pt}
The horizontal coordinate is the inlet molar ratio
\[
r_{H_2/CO}=\frac{F_{H_2,in}}{F_{CO,in}},
\]
where $r_{H_2/CO}=3$ is the stoichiometric feed ratio for CO methanation. The vertical coordinate is catalyst space time on a CO-feed basis:
\begin{keyequation}
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\tau_{cat,CO}=\frac{W_{cat}}{F_{CO,in}}}
\qquad [\tau_{cat,CO}]=\mathrm{kg_{cat}\,s\,mol^{-1}_{CO}}.
\]
\end{keyequation}
Here $W_{cat}$ is catalyst mass and $F_{CO,in}$ is inlet CO molar flow. This ratio has units of catalyst mass per CO throughput. A larger value means more catalyst is available per mole of CO fed. It is often informally called contact time, but it is not the gas residence time in seconds: gas residence time depends on bed void volume and the local volumetric flow, while $W_{cat}/F_{CO,in}$ is a catalyst-normalized throughput measure.

The sweep holds $W_{cat}=3.12\,\mathrm g$ fixed, so it changes space time by changing feed throughput. Equivalently,
\[
F_{CO,in}=\frac{W_{cat}}{\tau_{cat,CO}},\qquad
F_{H_2,in}=r_{H_2/CO}F_{CO,in},\qquad
F_{N_2,in}=\left(\frac{F_{N_2}}{F_{CO}}\right)_{Exp1}F_{CO,in}.
\]
Thus CO and N$_2$ flows scale together and H$_2$ is reset to the selected ratio; the N$_2$/CO ratio remains at its Experiment 1 value. All other selected inputs are held fixed at 350\,$^\circ$C, 1 bar absolute, and isothermal operation. The conversion plotted at each grid point is
\[
X_{CO,out}(\%)=100\left(1-\frac{F_{CO,out}}{F_{CO,in}}\right).
\]

The map shows the combined conditional response to stoichiometry and catalyst per CO throughput. Conversion rises strongly as the feed approaches the stoichiometric ratio from below, then the improvement diminishes at higher H$_2$/CO and larger space time. These are model sensitivities, not validated operating predictions: the 1 in $\times$ 12 in geometry is assumed, the 3.12 g charge implies a sparse-bed voidage of about 0.98384 under the assumed particle density, and 1 bar is below the kinetic fit's 5 bar lower limit. The source describes atmospheric pressure in its abstract and discussion while Appendix A states 2 bar; this sweep follows the atmospheric-pressure description using a 1 bar override.

\partdivider{III}{Optimization}{A separate four-factor Full M4 grid search. The reported result is the best sampled point, not a continuous or experimentally validated optimum.}
\section{Optimization}
The discrete grid search identifies the best sampled operating point for the Full M4 reactor model. It maximizes completed-bed CO conversion over four inputs: inlet H$_2$/CO ratio, inlet temperature, inlet pressure, and catalyst space time on a CO-feed basis. The Experiment 1 catalyst mass (3.12 g), bed geometry and voidage, and N$_2$/CO inlet ratio are fixed. At each selected H$_2$/CO ratio and space time, the inlet flows are reconstructed as
\[
F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}=\frac{W_{\mathrm{cat}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{cat,CO}}},\qquad
F_{\mathrm{H_2,in}}=r_{\mathrm{H_2/CO}}F_{\mathrm{CO,in}},\qquad
F_{\mathrm{N_2,in}}=\left(\frac{F_{\mathrm{N_2}}}{F_{\mathrm{CO}}}\right)_{\!\mathrm{Exp1}}F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}.
\]
Thus changing space time scales the inlet throughput while preserving N$_2$/CO; N$_2$ is not an independent optimization variable. Each point is an isothermal Full M4 simulation with Ergun pressure drop, solved using BDF. The grid contains 12 H$_2$/CO ratios, 9 inlet temperatures, 9 pressures, and 21 space-time values, for $12\times9\times9\times21=20{,}412$ simulations; all completed. The objective is
\[
\max_{r_{\mathrm{H_2/CO}},\,T_{in},\,P_{in},\,\tau_{\mathrm{cat,CO}}}
X_{\mathrm{CO,out}},\qquad
X_{\mathrm{CO,out}}=100\left(1-\frac{F_{\mathrm{CO,out}}}{F_{\mathrm{CO,in}}}\right)\%.
\]

The maximum is the best sampled grid point, not a continuous or experimentally validated optimum. The selected point lies at the upper bounds for H$_2$/CO, pressure, and space time. The objective does not penalize hydrogen use, low throughput, or operating pressure, and the model is isothermal. Treat the result as exploratory; a practical operating recommendation would require those constraints and objectives to be defined.
