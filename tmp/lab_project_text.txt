Mechanistic Modeling, Simulation, and Optimization of a Fixed-Bed Ni/Al2O3 Reactor for
                                   CO Methanation




Central Question
Can a computational model be developed from literature kinetics and reactor engineering
principles to reproduce the performance of the Ni/Al2O3 reactor observed in Experiment #1, and
can that model be used to predict and optimize reactor performance beyond the conditions tested
experimentally?
Objectives
The project aims to:
    • Develop a mechanistic model of the fixed-bed Ni/Al2O3 CO methanation reactor.
    • Predict reactor performance, including CO conversion and CH4/CO2 selectivity.
    • Predict species and temperature profiles along the catalyst bed.
    • Compare model predictions with the experimental results obtained in Experiment #1.
    • Investigate the effects of operating conditions such as temperature, feed composition, flow
        rate, pressure, and catalyst loading.
    • Examine the influence of kinetic parameters and catalyst activity on reactor performance.
    • Investigate non-isothermal behavior, temperature rise, and potential hot-spot formation.
    • Use the validated model to identify operating conditions that improve reactor performance
        while maintaining reasonable operating constraints.

Main Components
•       Reaction Kinetics and Thermodynamics
Literature investigation of CO methanation kinetics, Ni-based catalysts, equilibrium behavior,
and the origin of CO2 formation.
•       Reactor Modeling and Simulation
Development and numerical solution of a one-dimensional fixed-bed reactor model incorporating
species and energy balances.
•       Experimental Validation
Comparison of computational predictions with the group's experimental GC data from
Experiment #1.
•       Sensitivity and Parametric Analysis
Investigation of how operating conditions and model parameters influence conversion,
selectivity, and reactor temperature.
•       Deactivation and Thermal Behavior
Exploration of catalyst deactivation and the thermal characteristics of the highly exothermic
methanation reaction. + (catalyst deactivation - decay model)
•       Optimization
Use of the validated model to identify favorable operating conditions for achieving high CO
conversion and CH4/CO2 selectivity while respecting practical constraints.


 Overall progression
 Experiment → Modeling → Validation → Prediction → Analysis → Optimization
